About

Curiosity, with roots.

From Toledo, Ohio, to Stanford—and a few places in between.

A builder’s background.

I’m an electrical engineering student and political science minor at Stanford. I grew up in Toledo, Ohio, building things at the Toledo Technology Academy of Engineering, from CAD models to CNC-machined parts.

My work so far spans AI security, ice-penetrating radar, and sustainability research. My interests extend to robotics, software, and the ways hardware and intelligent systems interact. I enjoy moving between disciplines, from building a physical system to thinking through its wider implications.

Technology is part of a bigger story.

I’m interested in how technology connects with policy and social good: who benefits, who faces barriers, and how governance can keep pace with new capabilities. Environmental research is one part of that broader curiosity, alongside robotics, AI, and community-focused work.

Beyond the lab.

I spent three months studying abroad in Kyoto in spring 2026, exploring a different perspective on technology, community, and policy while developing my Japanese to the N4 level.

My interests also include piano, coding, and the Stanford Student Space Initiative. Through Proyecto Vidas Valiosas, I connect Stanford students with nonprofits addressing homelessness, food insecurity, and education access.
