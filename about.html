About

A little about me.

I’m from Toledo, Ohio. These days, I study electrical engineering and political science at Stanford.

I started with things I could build.

At the Toledo Technology Academy of Engineering, I worked on CAD models and CNC-machined parts. At Stanford, that interest has taken me into radar hardware, programming, and AI security.

I like getting into the details of a technical problem. I also want to understand what happens around it: who uses the system, what can go wrong, and which decisions matter. That’s part of why I’m studying political science alongside engineering.

There’s more I want to explore.

I’m interested in robotics and the point where software meets physical hardware. I also care about environmental research and the practical ways technology can help people. I’m still figuring out how those interests will fit together in my career.

Outside coursework.

I founded Proyecto Vidas Valiosas to connect Stanford students with local nonprofits. I also play piano, am interested in the Stanford Student Space Initiative, and spent spring 2026 studying in Kyoto.

For a more personal introduction, I wrote “Stem Education” for Stanford Magazine. It’s about where I come from and finding room for the things I care about while studying engineering.
